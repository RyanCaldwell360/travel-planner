## E01 — Motivation

Ryan and Lily were discussing planning next year's travel. He wants to know PTO at any point, enter known later travel, and back-calculate what remains before it. February travel must fit around conferences, Greece, and Cozumel in September–November. Purpose: “a tool to identify opportunity and track actual usage.”

## E02 — Primary interface and policies

Quick visual PTO balances are useful, but trip opportunities are the main interface. Both accrue PTO per pay period. Ryan said “I believe” both get five weeks annually and can roll over 40 hours: tentative, not verified.

## E03 — Different people within a trip

Greece is Lily's trip. Cozumel involves both. Ryan may arrive a week before the Ironman; Lily could arrive Friday. Ryan will work remotely during the lead-up week, so travel duration must not equal PTO use. Users should click into a trip to specify each person's PTO.

## E04 — Flexible versus fixed

Users play with dates and trips. If something does not fit, the app should identify what must budge and where the time comes from. A trip or its individual days can be unalterable. The app should show options drawing from one or multiple other flexible trips. No elaborate hard rules are needed initially; they judge whether the changed trip is worthwhile.

## E05 — Family opportunities

Kaia's school breaks are good family travel opportunities. Parenting weekends are not strong constraints because her dad is flexible about swaps. Highlight breaks; do not impose custody restrictions.

## E06 — Whole horizon and alternatives

Ryan affirmed checking whether February leaves enough for later commitments. No minimum PTO cushion is needed. They want multiple saved alternatives. One shared plan with named versions is an acceptable starting point; exact comparison views require seeing the UI.

## E07 — Devices and durability

They would plan together on a laptop. Both must be able to edit from their own laptops. Edits must be saved, accessible to both, and survive closing the app. The year plan should be “clearly marked for us to review at any point.”

## E08 — Discovery, weather, cost

Start by choosing a location and each person's date range, informing the PTO landscape. Map exploration should help discover destinations with estimated cost and weather. Prices should come from sources, not their guesses. MSP is their base. Flights plus lodging are sufficient initial cost scope. They judge affordability themselves; the app need not make that decision.

## E09 — Inputs and priorities

Manual current-balance adjustments are acceptable. PTO view runs through year end. First implementation priority: explore/compare a year of travel with clear PTO tradeoffs, rather than deep itinerary editing.

## E10 — Work and aesthetics

Both work 40-hour Monday–Friday weeks. Lily must use PTO for holidays. Ryan chose all three visual influences offered: illustrated travel journal, polished travel magazine/photography, and map-led explorer.

## E11 — Current deliverable

Ryan asked for Google's OKF-format documentation encoding the app and its goals so Codex can begin implementation. This request is for a handoff, not authorization to claim an app has already been built.
